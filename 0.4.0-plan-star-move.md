# 0.4.0 — Star Movement (kinematic orbits, global star time)

The galaxy is frozen today: every star is a fixed `StarPacked` record and the only
motion is the camera's. 0.4.0 makes the galaxy *alive* — stars move on predefined
orbits, driven by one global star time the user scrubs with a slider:

```
slider −1 …        movement coupled to the camera fly speed ("time flows as you travel")
near 0             no movement
0.5 … 99           time speed in Myr/s (million years per second)
```

Constraints, explicit:

- **No gravity.** No force integration, no N-body, no potential. Every orbit is a
  closed-form function `position(record, T)`. Revisiting `T` gives the identical
  galaxy — the same reproducibility rule as the hash-based generator.
- **No record growth if possible.** The 16-byte `StarPacked` layout has survived
  every feature so far; §1.3 shows movement fits in the bits already spare.
- Movement must not change the *distribution*: the galaxy is the density field, and
  the field's shape must be preserved by the motion (§1.4 — the wobble-consistency
  rule). The one exception is spiral winding (§3.3), which is real physics and is
  analysed rather than hidden.

Dependency: 0.4.0 reads `GalaxyModel.dynamics` (the stub reserved in 0.3.0) and the
population info from `star-types.js` to pick orbit families. It can land on the Milky
Way preset alone with `dynamics` hard-coded to the same numbers.

## 1. The orbit model

One law for everything, with per-star parameters. Let `C` be the model centre
(`GALACTIC_CENTRE` for the Milky Way preset, the origin for other types) and
`q = p₀ − C` the star's galactocentric offset at `T = 0`:

```
p(T) = C + Rot_z(θ(T)) · ( q + w(T) − w(0) )

θ(T)  = ω_sel · T            ω_sel from the star's family (below)
w(T)  = Σᵢ Aᵢ · sin(ωᵢ T + ψᵢ) · êᵢ      per-family wobble basis (below)
```

The `w(T) − w(0)` form is the **T = 0 identity rule**: at `T = 0` the transform is
exactly `p₀`, bit-for-bit the static renderer's position. This is a testable
invariant and the regression guard for the whole feature.

### 1.1 The rotation law per family

| family | who | ω_sel | wobble basis êᵢ, ωᵢ | amplitudes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pattern` | young population (O/B, OB-association members), gas nebulae, arm objects | `Ω_p`, the group speed derived per model (§1.1a) — the pattern rotates rigidly, the stars riding it do not have to | none | 0 |
| `disc` | thin + thick disc, old population | `Ω(r) = v(r)/max(r, rCore)` — the local **group rate** of the stars at that radius. Differential, no lock (§1.1a, revised 0.4.3) | radial + vertical epicycle at `κ(R) ≈ √2·Ω(R)` (radial along the star's own radius) | linear rank × `2σThin/κ`, vertical 0.2×, capped by the disc height |
| `bar` | spheroid of barred types | the pattern `Ω_p`: the star's **seat** — the loop centre — rides the bar. The star itself does not (§1.5) | x1 loop about the seat: radial, at `Ω_stream(r) = Ω(r) − Ω_p` the rate at which the star laps the pattern | linear rank × 0.15 × `pressureAmpScale` (4.5% of the bar's semimajor axis at the cap) |
| `pressure` | spheroid of E/S0, halo, all of E galaxies | slow net spin `λ·ω̄` (λ ∈ 0.05–0.3 from `dynamics.spinLambda`; ω̄ = mean **disc** frequency Ω(r) for disc types, Keplerian `0.05·r^-1.5` only when the type has no disc curve — §1.1 curve block) | radial + vertical boil at the local clock `ω̄(r)` | linear rank × `2σ_sph/ω̄(1 kpc)`, bulge-capped (§1.4) |

### 1.1a The group speed (pattern speed), derived per model — revised 0.4.3

**What the first two cuts got wrong.** 0.4.1 answered "the bar is slower than the
stars near it" by locking the whole inner disc to `Ω_p` (the *group zone*), and
0.4.2 gave every barred type a pattern speed. Both are still one rigid speed for
bar + arms + belt, and the next report was exact: *"bar rotate as rigid shape.
stars on different distance from center has same angular speed — this is
impossible."* A rigid body is not what a bar is: the pattern is rigid, the stars
are not.

**The physics** (Kormendy's density-wave introduction; Harsoula et al. 2021 for the
orbit picture): a bar or a spiral is a *density wave*. Its shape rotates rigidly at
the pattern speed `Ω_p`, but the stars live on closed orbits whose apsides precess
at `Ω − κ/2`, each star still circulating at its own `Ω(r)`. The pattern is
supported because a *group* of stars has orbits whose precession has the same mean
rate and whose orientations line up. Pattern speed = that group's mean precession
rate:

```
Ω_p = ⟨ Ω − κ/2 ⟩        kappa = √2·Ω on a flat curve
      = (1 − 1/√2) · ⟨Ω⟩        (same relation the epicycle law already uses)
```

read **where the pattern is set**:

- **bar**: at the bar's end (`a·r0`, cut by `barTipRadius`). The ends define the
  mode — the fast-bar condition — and a mass-weighted average over the whole body
  would instead be dominated by the inner kiloparsecs and land 2–3× above every
  measured bar.
- **arms**: averaged over the thin disc's own radial mass profile (`discRadialWeight`),
  because a spiral is a disc-wide wave, not a body: `(1 − 1/√2)·⟨Ω⟩_disc`.
- **no arms, no pattern**: `Ω_p = 0` (S0/E/Irr-base), so those models carry no
  pattern family at all; Irr's clumps carry their own slow spin through the same
  arithmetic mean of a 2-3 m profile.

Nothing is authored: `galaxy.js` derives `Ω_p` from the model's own curve and shape
(`patternSpeed`, Simpson 1024 bins), so the preset, the table types and any override
cannot disagree. Derived values (km/s/kpc): SB0 40.7, SBa 31.8, SBb 43.0, SBc 37.9,
SBd 53.0 — all inside the observed 33–55 bar band, the Milky Way's own bulge at the
middle of its 33–41 range; unbarred Sa–Sd 11–20; Irr 5.3. Corotation comes out at
3.4 bar lengths for every barred type (5.1 kpc on the SBb preset, 5.5 on SB0) —
beyond the bar end by construction, which is the fast-bar geometry.

**The disc is no longer locked.** `disc` runs at `Ω(r)` everywhere. The lock existed
only to hide the shear between a rigid bar and its neighbours, and the shear is not
an artifact to hide: inside corotation the stars *do* overtake the bar (that is what
builds the density), and outside it they fall behind. `patternLock` survives as the
documented cosmetic escape hatch (one flag, rigid everywhere, still a no-op without
a pattern). The seam the lock used to make continuous is gone with it: there is only
one branch.

Rotation curves per type live in `dynamics` (kpc, Myr):

```
disc types:  v(R) = vFlat · R / max(R, rCore)          // flat beyond the core, solid inside
             Milky Way preset: vFlat = 0.225 kpc/Myr (≈ 220 km/s), rCore = 0.5 kpc
             Ω(R) = 0.225 / max(R, 0.5)  rad/Myr
MW pattern:  Ω_p = 0.0430 rad/Myr (43.0 km/s/kpc) — derived at the bulge's own radius
             (a·r0 = 1.5 kpc); pattern period 146 Myr, corotation R_CR = vFlat/Ω_p = 5.12 kpc
disc-type spheroids (pressure): ω̄ = Ω(R) of the curve above; bulk = λ·Ω(R)
             with λ = dynamics.spinLambda ∈ 0.05–0.3 (λ = 0 froze S0/spiral
             spheroids — the "half the stars don't move" bug)
E / spheroid: no flat curve — pressure family; ω̄ ≈ 0.05 rad/Myr at 1 kpc, ∝ r^(−1.5)
             outside (Keplerian clock, purely for oscillation frequencies); Ω_p = 0
Irr:         vFlat ≈ 0.05 kpc/Myr (≈ 50 km/s), rCore ≈ 2 kpc (rising curve), clump
             members use `pattern` with the clump's own slow spin
```

Reference periods (what the user sees turning):

| orbit | period | at 0.5 Myr/s | at 99 Myr/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| solar circle, R = 8.2 kpc | 229 Myr | 0.8 °/s | 156 °/s |
| bar / arm pattern | 146 Myr | 1.2 °/s | 245 °/s |
| inner disc, R = 1 kpc (**no longer locked** — O/B orbit) | 28 Myr | 6.4 °/s | (strobe) |
| thin-disc epicycle at the Sun (κ) | 162 Myr | 1.1 °/s | 225 °/s |
| halo orbit, r ≈ 10 kpc | ~600 Myr | 0.3 °/s | 60 °/s |

The inner-disc row is the honest one now: a star at 1 kpc genuinely orbits 5× faster
than the pattern. It is *not* a rigid galaxy, and at 99 Myr/s it is a strobe (the
existing sub-pixel fade damps it; §4). Before 0.4.1 the same row read 28 Myr *with*
the bar at 154 — which was the 0.4.1 bug report: the bar seemed to lag. It does not
lag: the bar's *pattern* is at 43 km/s/kpc, and the stars the user sees streaming
around and through it are at their own Ω(r). That is the density wave.

### 1.2 Family assignment (generation time)

`star-types.js` already derives class and age per star. The family is a pure function
of that plus the component:

```
derived class is O/B/A  OR  member of an object (0.3.2) with age < 100 Myr   → pattern
component thin/thick                                                          → disc
component bulge (barred type)                                                 → bar
component bulge (unbarred) or halo                                            → pressure
```

The Milky Way's real catalog stars and landmarks: family from their spectral class
(O/B/A → pattern, else disc) — one helper, `familyFromColorIndex`, owns that rule so
the record writers (landmarks at load, catalog cells at decode — the shipped bundle
predates 0.4, so the loader stamps families on decode), `label-layer.js` and
`selection.js` can never disagree. The Sun and Sirius ride `disc`, so the Sun drifts along
its real orbit and the constellations slowly deform. That is honest and it makes the
time slider do something memorable to the labels.

### 1.3 Record bits: the 16-byte layout survives

Per star the law needs: family (2 bits), epicycle phase `ψ` (4 bits is enough — visual
phase quantisation 22.5°), amplitude rank (4 bits, linear-quantiled — a Rayleigh `ln`/`sqrt`
per vertex bought nothing visible and two transcendentals the SFU budget already spends
on sin/cos, so the rank maps linearly: `A = rank_frac · A_max` with `rank_frac = (u+0.5)/16`).
The `flags` byte of
`packPacked` today spends bit 0 on visible, bit 1 on landmark, bit 2 on variable, so five
bits are free — **`StarPacked` stays 16 bytes**:

```
flags  bits 3–4   orbit family (pattern / disc / bar / pressure)
flags  bits 5–7   spare (future: binary flag, etc.)
jitter bits 0–3   epicycle phase ψ = k·2π/16
jitter bits 4–7   amplitude rank u → A = ((u+0.5)/16) · A_max   (A_max = 2σ/κ or the
                   pressure scale below; 16 linear levels, per-vertex cost: one multiply)
```

Two of the three visible/landmark/variable bits are load-bearing in `star-record.js` and the
tile encoders, so the family pair is the only contiguous room in the record; if 0.4 ever
needs a third bit it takes it from `jitter`, not from a new field, and `export-parity-test`
is what catches a CPU/GPU disagreement about that.

`star-record.js` grows `packOrbit()`/`readOrbit()` alongside `packPacked()`, and the
WGSL decode is a shift-and-select. Generation (CPU, at build / gap-fill / catalog-write
time) fills the bits from `(seed, index)` — same stable-hash rule as everything else.
Note `jitter` is documented as "sub-cell position jitter / spare" — this *is* the spare
being used; the sub-cell jitter was never decoded on the GPU (worklog known issue), so
the byte is reclaimed and the doc comment updated.

### 1.5 The bar's stars stream through the pattern (0.4.3)

A rigid bar family is what made the bar read as a solid object. The fix is one
added term, not a new family: every `bar` star keeps its sampled position as a
**seat on the pattern** (rotating at `Ω_p`) and circulates about that seat on an
x1 loop at the rate it laps the pattern:

```
stream = Ω(r) − Ω_p          r = the star's own galactocentric radius
A_loop = rank × 0.15 × pressureAmpScale     (4.5% of the bar's semimajor axis at the cap)
w_loop(T) = A_loop · ( sinTau(ψ + stream·T) − sinTau(ψ) ) · ê_r
```

Three properties make this the right shape for the visual bug:

1. **Speed varies with radius, per group.** `stream` is a function of `r`, not of the
   star: 0.188 rad/Myr at 1 kpc on SB0, 0.063 at 2 kpc, 0 at corotation, reversed
   beyond it. Stars near the centre lap the bar fastest, stars near the end drift with
   it, stars past corotation stream backwards through it — the density wave's
   signature, and the direct inverse of "stars at different distances have the same
   angular speed".
2. **Still one group, still a bar.** The seat rotates at the *derived group speed*
   (§1.1a), the loop amplitude is a fraction of the bar's own cap, and `T = 0` is the
   identity. Measured on a 400k-star SB0 sample in the bar's own frame: the 90%
   outline of the bar stars stays `0.62 × a` long and `0.85 × b` wide over 400 Myr
   (1% drift) while the disc rounds outside it — the shape cannot be torn.
3. **Performance: one sin pair.** The loop is the same closed form as the disc's
   epicycle (one more `sinTau`, one multiply, one `select`-free branch), and the disc
   branch *loses* the corotation select. Vertex-ALU net change is ≈ 0 (§4).

Amplitudes were chosen by measurement, not taste: 0.15–0.5 × `pressureAmpScale` all
hold the outline (the 90% outline of the bar stars drifts 0.2–0.5% at 0.15 and
0.8–1.3% at 0.5 over 400 Myr), so the fraction was set by what the *streaming*
needs to be visible — 0.15 keeps the disc's shell pattern at its own radii while
costing half the outline blur of 0.25. The seat offset also stops the barycentre of
a radially uniform bar from spreading (bare uniform-rank loops move the centre of mass
by `0.04 × A` — 65 pc on SB0); with the seat anchoring it, the measured centroid stays
within 1.5% of the bar radius.

### 1.4 Wobble-consistency rule (distribution preservation)

A wobble amplitude that contradicts the density model makes the galaxy *breathe*: a
star sampled from `rho` at `p₀` wanders into regions where `rho` is lower and the
time-averaged field no longer matches the analytic distribution. Rules:

1. **Rotation preserves everything** — `Rot_z` is measure-preserving for the axisymmetric
   part of `rho`, and at `T = 0` the full field (arms included) is exactly the sampled
   distribution. No constraint needed on `ω_sel` for distribution preservation.
2. **Wobble amplitudes must match the population's velocity dispersion and nothing
   more.** The amplitude tables are `A = σ / ω` evaluated from `dynamics` — the same
   numbers the population's velocity dispersion implies. `amplitude rank` spans
   0–2σ. Vertical wobble is hard-capped: `|z₀| + A_z < truncation.discHeight` (clip
   the amplitude, do not clamp the position — clamping piles stars on the boundary).
3. `experiments/orbit-test.js` holds the law itself: T = 0 identity, periods, the
   group-zone continuity, CPU-vs-f32-WGSL replay. The distribution-preservation
   half of this rule — generate 200k stars, replay T = 0…400 Myr in 10 Myr steps,
   compare time-averaged radial/vertical histograms against the analytic profile
   (3% per bin) — lands with the amplitude-calibration pass: it is the gate that
   lets the amplitude tables be tuned, and the tables are not final until then.

Default amplitudes (Milky Way preset, 1σ rank): thin disc (x_a, z_a) = (0.6, 0.12) kpc
at the Sun, scaling with σ/κ(R); thick (0.9, 0.35); bar loop (0.3, 0.15). One worked
correction to keep in mind when filling `dynamics` for spheroids: the raw virial
estimate `A = σ_v/ω̄` for a MW bulge (σ_v ≈ 0.15 kpc/Myr, ω̄ ≈ 0.05 rad/Myr) gives
A ≈ 3 kpc — honest for an E-galaxy's pressure-supported body, absurd for a 1.5 kpc
bulge inside a disc. Bulge `pressure` amplitudes are therefore capped at 0.3 of the
spheroid's own axis in that direction (an explicit, documented "visual dynamical
friction"); E-galaxy `pressure` stars use the unscaled σ/ω values — their orbits
really are that wild and it reads as pressure support.

## 2. Architecture: one shader, not two

The question was: if the movement is heavy, use a separate shader with movement and
without — which architecture is better? Three candidates:

| option | frozen-frame cost | live-frame cost | complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. one shader, position law always evaluated** (recommended) | ~10 ALU + 2 SFU per vertex over today (frozen = constant T; the same transform re-evaluates every frame) | same +10 ALU + 2 SFU | one pipeline; law is a `SHADER_PARTS['orbit']` mirror like density |
| B. one shader, `if (timeMode) …` uniform branch | ~0 (skipped) | same as A | branch must stay *uniform*; a future per-star mode flag would diverge and silently wreck it |
| C. two pipelines (static / orbit), swapped per frame | exactly today's shader | exactly A's | two 100-line vertex shaders to keep in sync; `wgsl-validate`/`wgsl-exec` matrix doubles |

**Decision: A.** The movement math is two transcendentals and a rotation per vertex
against a vertex stage that already does `log2`, `exp2`, a matrix transform and a
texture fetch — §4 measures the increment at ≤ 25% of the vertex stage and ≤ 10% of
frame time at 2M stars. A frozen frame is not a different workload: `T` stops
advancing and the law evaluates the same constant transform every frame. Paying
10 ALU to keep **one** shader, one pipeline, one validation surface is the right
trade; option B's uniform-branch "savings" are noise, and its hidden trap is that the
day someone wants *some* stars frozen the branch becomes divergent. Option C stays a
mechanical escape hatch: `STAR_SPRITE` is assembled from parts already, so
`SHADERS['star-sprite-static'] = BASE` and `SHADERS['star-sprite-orbit'] = ORBIT + BASE`
cost one concat line each — activate it only if the motion-budget experiment (§4)
shows the law above 15% of frame time on the target device.

Structure of the shader change (and why the mirror convention makes it cheap):

```
SHADER_PARTS['orbit']    fn starPosition(rec: StarPacked, T: f32) -> vec3f
                         — the exact law of §1, mirroring src/math/orbit.js
SHADER_PARTS['star-sprite']  becomes BASE (structs, decode, vs/fs) with the vs calling
                         starPosition(star, camera.params.w) instead of reading xyz directly
SHADERS['star-sprite']   = ORBIT + BASE        (WGSL has no includes; concat as with
                                                pcg-hash + density today)
```

`camera.params.w` is the `w` of `params: vec4f // x magZero, y baseSizePx, z maxSizePx, w
star time` — the struct comment already reserves it, and today `uniform[27] = time` holds the rAF
loop's seconds, which no star-shader line reads (the `params.w < 0.5` SDR branch in
`shaders.js` is the *tonemap* uniform, a different buffer). **Amendment (0.4.1): the uniform
also grows by two vec4s, `dynA`/`dynB`** — the per-model orbit numbers (vFlat, rCore,
ω_p, spin | σ_thin, pressure amplitude scale, disc height, patternLock), packed each frame by
`orbit.packOrbitDynamics`. The original "hard-code the Milky Way's constants in WGSL" shortcut
broke as soon as a second galaxy type was drawn (the GPU kept 0.041/0.225 while the CPU mirror
read the model) — 32 bytes per frame is the cheaper mistake. Uniform: 112 → 144 bytes,
`UNIFORM_FLOATS` 28 → 36, and `renderer-test.js`'s
'uniform carries the frame time' becomes 'uniform carries the star time' — that check is the only
consumer of the old value and must move with it, not be kept alongside. A rejected alternative, for the record:
a compute pre-pass writing transformed positions each frame (1 eval per star instead of
4 per quad). It wins past ~10M stars and costs a pass, a 16 B write + 16 B read per
star per frame (32 MB/frame at 2M = 1.9 GB/s at 60 fps) and a second buffer to keep in
sync. Not needed at this feature's budgets.

Per-star family selection is decode-and-select data flow (a 2-bit switch that picks
`ω_sel` and three amplitudes), not a divergent shader branch: all stars run the *same*
formula, with `pattern` simply having zero wobble amplitudes and a constant ω.

## 3. Orbit distribution in different galaxies

"What is the orbit distribution in different galaxies" — answered per 0.3 type. The
table is the `dynamics` + family-fraction content of `GalaxyModel` — the field list 0.3.0 carries as a
stub and 0.4 fills in is `dynamics.{vFlat, rCore, omegaPattern, sigmaThin, sigmaThick,
sigmaSpheroid, spinLambda, loopRatio, patternLock}`, in kpc/Myr throughout —
`omegaPattern` and `loopRatio` derived from the model's own geometry (§1.1a), never
authored:

| type | pattern | disc | bar | pressure | law | σ_v (km/s) | how it reads on the slider |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0–E7 | 0 | 0 | 0 (no bar) | 1 (net spin fraction λ = 0.05–0.3 rises toward E7) | pressure: anisotropic oscillation, σ_r > σ_θ (β ≈ 0.3), slow whole-body spin λ·ω̄ | 300 (E0 giants) → 150 (E7) | *no disc rotation at all*: boiling on a ~20–50 Myr clock plus a slow visible spin. At 0.5 Myr/s it breathes; at 99 Myr/s it shimmers (strobe note, §4) |
| S0 | 0 | 0.7 | 0.2 if SB0 | 0.3 | v(R) flat, vFlat ≈ 0.23; no arms → Ω_p = 0 | 40–60 | smooth differential rotation; nothing winds (an axisymmetric field stays axisymmetric) |
| Sa–Sd | 0.05–0.15 (rises with stage — more young stars) | 0.8–0.9 | 0 (unbarred) | 0.05 | vFlat 0.20–0.24, rCore 0.3–1; derived Ω_p 11–20 km/s/kpc (§1.1a) | thin 25–40, thick 40–60 | differential everywhere; the arm pattern and the young stars pinned to it (§3.3) |
| SBa–SBd | 0.05–0.15 | 0.6–0.8 | 0.1–0.2 | 0.05 | as above + derived Ω_p 32–53 km/s/kpc (bar end), bar length 0.2–0.5 disc radius | bar 80–150 | the bar's pattern turns as one group, its stars stream through it at Ω(r) − Ω_p (§1.5), the disc is differential |
| Irr | 0.2 (clump members) | 0.7 (rising-curve disc) | 0 | 0.1 | vFlat ≈ 0.05, rCore ≈ 2 (rising) | 20–30 | slow swirl + clump members orbiting inside their clumps; reads "alive", never structured |

Fractions are by generated star count at the default budgets; they sum to ≈ 1.

### 3.1 Family fractions follow populations, not free parameters

The table's fractions are consequences of 0.3.3's SFH at the chosen `age`: a young Sc
has more `pattern` stars; an old Sc's disc is entirely `disc` (the O/B have died).
`familyAssignment` (§1.2) derives this per star — the table is a cross-check target
for `orbit-test.js`, not a knob.

### 3.2 Epicyclic structure across a disc

For a flat curve `κ = √2·Ω`, so epicycle periods are `2π/κ = 0.707 ×` the local
rotation period and the wobble keeps pace with the shear at every R. Radial amplitude
`σ_R/κ(R)` *grows* with R (κ falls) while the density falls — outer-disc stars get the
livelier wobble, exactly like the real velocity ellipsoid. Inside `rCore` the
solid-body law keeps Ω finite (no singularity at the centre) and κ → 2Ω.

### 3.3 Spiral winding — analysed, not hidden

Differential rotation winds spiral structure, and after 0.4.3 the whole disc is
differential (the lock is gone). What winds is the *material*: the density-wave
pattern itself does not, because the pattern is the aligned seats and they turn at
Ω_p. A `disc` star at R = 12 kpc runs at Ω = 0.019 rad/Myr while the pattern runs
0.043 — relative shear 0.024 rad/Myr ≈ 1.4°/Myr — so the material that is *not*
pinned to a seat shears away from the seat it was sampled on, and the arm contrast
fades where the shear has carried the stars off the ridge. Consequences by slider
zone:

- **0.5–5 Myr/s** (the interesting zone): the outer arms soften and slowly shear
  over minutes of watching while the bar's seats hold the bar's shape, and the
  bar's stars visibly stream through it. This is real galactic dynamics and a
  feature.
- **99 Myr/s**: the outer arms wind into rings in seconds; the bar, its streaming
  stars, the young population and the gas stay coherent. The galaxy relaxes to
  "coherent core + sheared outer material" — the density-wave picture.

Mitigation for users who want a pretty, non-winding galaxy at any radius:
`dynamics.patternLock` (false by default) forces `ω_sel = Ω_p` for the `disc`
family at *all* radii (rigid rotation at the pattern speed; it is a no-op when
Ω_p = 0). One flag, documented as "cosmetic — turns off differential
rotation". Path C density quads (when they land) rotate with the same family law for
their cell's dominant component so the glow never shears against the points; the arm
modulation of a Path C cell follows `pattern` (cells carrying arm contrast *are* the
pattern, exactly like the young population).

## 4. Budget: how many stars can move on a typical RTX 3050

Reference device: laptop RTX 3050 — GA107, 2048 CUDA cores, 128-bit GDDR6
(192–224 GB/s), 4.5–9 TFLOPS FP32. Derate for realistic clocks and occupancy: ≈ 1.5
TFLOP/s and 60 Gpix/s effective blended/alpha-tested fill. The analysis is op-counts ×
throughput; `experiments/motion-budget.js` (a study, like `packing-test.js`) computes
the same table so the numbers can be re-derived rather than trusted.

Per moving star per frame (4-vertex instanced quad):

| stage | today | + movement | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| vertex ALU | ~320 FLOP (80 × 4 verts) | +80 (rotation 6, law/decode 14 per vert) | 400 FLOP |
| vertex SFU | 8 (log2, exp2 × 4 verts) | +8 (sin, cos × 4 verts; the 2-bit family select adds none; the bar's §1.5 loop is the same one sin pair the disc already pays, and dropping the corotation lock removes a select) | 16 |
| vertex texture | 4 LUT fetches | 0 | 4 |
| bandwidth | 16 B record, L1-warm across 4 verts | 0 extra | 16 B |
| fragment | ~6 px² mean coverage × 20 FLOP, falloff³ + discard | 0 (motion does not change sprite size) | 120 FLOP |

The *increment* at 60 fps, from those two derates and nothing else — 80 ALU + 8 SFU per star
per frame, ALU and SFU summed because a vertex stage issues both from one warp:

| drawn stars | movement ALU | movement SFU | increment | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300k (today's default scene) | 0.02 ms | 0.03 ms | ≈ 0.05 ms | invisible |
| 1M (catalog target, plan.md §12) | 0.05 ms | 0.11 ms | ≈ 0.16 ms | invisible |
| 2M | 0.11 ms | 0.22 ms | ≈ 0.33 ms | invisible |
| 5M (synthetic resident target) | 0.27 ms | 0.56 ms | ≈ 0.8 ms | still small |
| 10M | 0.53 ms | 1.1 ms | ≈ 1.7 ms | the law is not what breaks |

Absolute milliseconds are only as trustworthy as the derate factor, which is a guess about a
driver, a clock and an occupancy — `motion-budget.js` therefore reports the *ratio* (the
reliable part: +25% vertex ALU, +100% vertex SFU, 0 extra bandwidth) and this table next to a
measured frame-time delta on the device it runs on. The worklog entry for 0.4 records the
measured number; re-deriving this table by hand is not evidence.

**Answer: every star that is drawn can move.** +25% on a vertex stage that was already doing
4× that work is not a limit anyone reaches; the limits are the ones the engine already has —
instance count, sprite fill and CPU-side generation/streaming of records. Fill deserves the
warning it does not usually get: at `BASE_SIZE_PX` a 2M-star field covers 12 Mpx/frame
(0.2 ms), which is nothing, but a sprite at `MAX_SIZE_PX` 22 px is 484 px² and 10% of the
field at that size is 97 Mpx (1.6 ms) — the fill cliff is zoom, not count, and that is Path
C's reason to exist rather than the movement law's. Rule of thumb for the budget sliders of
the future: **1M moving stars at 60 fps is the comfort zone on a 3050-class GPU, 2M is safe,
5M needs the LOD story to be finished.** Worked example for the current buffers: 300k global
+ 20k gap-fill + 20k objects + 250k catalog = 590k moving instances ≈ 0.2 ms of added vertex
work — nobody will notice the cost.

CPU side: integrating T is one f64 add per frame and one uniform float upload.
Family/bit packing happens at generation time only.

**Strobe caveat** (not cost — sampling): at 99 Myr/s the inner disc crosses many pixels
per frame — at R = 1 kpc it reaches 1273 °/s, which is *real* (that star orbits in
28 Myr) and the price of not locking the belt to the pattern again. Sub-pixel sprites
alias into flicker. Mitigations: the existing sprite size floor (0.8 px) and sub-pixel
fade already damp it; further (motion-stretch quads, temporal accumulation) is out of
scope. The overlay shows the rate so nobody thinks it is a bug. The bar's own stars are
NOT in this category: their seats turn at Ω_p (245 °/s at max rate) and their loop is
only the pattern-frame residual, so the bar reads smooth at any slider position — that
is the point of §1.5.

## 5. Precision: f32 angles over long sessions

Positions are f32 anyway (camera-relative); the risk is `sin/cos` of a large product
`ω·T` losing meaning. WGSL `sin` of large arguments is implementation-defined, so the
law **always reduces first**:

```
θ  = ω_sel · T
θr = 6.2831853 · fract(θ · 0.15915494)      // reduce mod 2π before sin/cos
```

f32 error budget (mantissa 2⁻²⁴, worst at the product before `fract`):

| session | T reached | worst θ error (inner disc) | position error at R = 1 kpc | at 10 kpc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 h at 99 Myr/s | 3.6e5 Myr | ~3e-3 rad | 3 pc | 30 pc (sub-pixel) |
| 10 h at 99 Myr/s | 3.6e6 Myr | ~3e-2 rad | 30 pc | 300 pc (≈ 1 px at 10 kpc) |

Mitigation: the CPU wraps `T` to `[0, 2²³)` Myr (8.4e6 Myr ≈ 36 000 Milky Way
rotations; the recycle point at max rate is a 23-hour session, at 1 Myr/s it is a
265-year session). A wrap is one global, documented discontinuity ("new galactic
epoch") and bounds every error in the table. `experiments/orbit-precision-test.js`
replays the f64 truth against the f32 shader arithmetic (JS `Math.fround` mirror) and
pins the bounds; if long max-rate sessions ever need to run *without* the wrap, the
escalation is a two-part time uniform (`T_hi` epoch counter + `T_lo`) — not needed
while the wrap covers the use case.

The T = 0 identity rule (§1) is independent of this: `fract(0) = 0` exactly.

## 6. The star time slider

Menu (Tab) gains a **time** section:

```
star time   [ ⟨————●————⟩  follow ×1.0 ]     value label: follow / frozen / "+8.0 Myr/s"
[T]         freeze / resume to the last non-zero rate
```

Exact mapping — slider value `s ∈ [−1, 99]`, `step 0.5`:

| s | meaning | dT/dt |
|---|---|---|
| −1 | camera-coupled, full | `FLIGHT_TIME_GAIN · speedLyPerSec` — 0.125 Myr per ly flown, so the default 8 ly/s cruise advances 1 Myr/s |
| −0.5 | camera-coupled, half | 0.5 × the above |
| 0 | frozen | 0 — the step is 0.5, so 0 is landed on exactly; no near-zero snap threshold, and none should be added |
| 0.5 … 99 | explicit rate | `s` Myr/s |

Semantics:

- **Camera coupling** (`−1 … −0.5`): time flows with distance flown — `T +=
  FLIGHT_TIME_GAIN · speedLyPerSec · dt`. The galaxy ages as you travel; stop flying
  and it freezes. In orbit modes the camera's linear speed is 0 by construction, so
  coupled mode is frozen there (documented in the help text). `FLIGHT_TIME_GAIN` is
  one named constant in `orbit.js` with its tuning note: chosen so the visible churn
  at cruise (≈ 1.57 °/s at the solar circle) matches the bottom of the explicit range.
- **Frozen** (`0`): `T` holds its value. Positions hold. Screenshot mode.
- **Explicit** (`0.5 … 99`): linear in Myr/s as specified. 0.5 Myr/s is the
  "watchable" floor (solar circle turns 360° in 7.6 min); 99 is the "see eons" end
  (360° in 2.3 s, winding §3.3 plays out in seconds).
- `T` is accumulated in **f64** (`starTimeMyr`) and exported to the GPU as one f32
  (the wrap of §5 applied at the export). Rate changes take effect the same frame; the
  accumulator is continuous across them. `MAX_FRAME_DT` clamps tab-switch spikes as
  today.
- The negative range is deliberately *not* reverse time — it is the camera-coupled
  zone, per spec. Reverse time (a symmetric `−0.5 … −99` Myr/s on a second row) is a
  trivial extension left out of 0.4.0.
- Overlay line: `star time frozen` / `star time +8.0 Myr/s` / `star time follow ×1.0`.
  The drawn-stars line is unchanged.

## 7. Everything that must move together

Points alone moving would shear against every other layer. The law (§1) is used by:

1. **Star sprites** — in the vertex shader via `starPosition` (the law is per-vertex
   work done 4× per star, accepted like the rest of the vertex stage).
2. **Landmarks + constellations + labels + picking** — `src/math/orbit.js` mirrors the
   WGSL exactly (the `SHADER_PARTS` validation pattern extended to the orbit law: the
   validator diffs formulas, `wgsl-exec` checks a rotated fixture numerically).
   `label-layer.js` and `selection.js` call `orbitPosition()` per landmark per frame —
   60 objects, f64, no allocation (scratch `Float64Array(3)` reused).
3. **Catalog + gap-fill records** — same bits at write time (§1.3); the shader does
   the rest. The shipped catalog bundle predates 0.4, so `tile-loader.decodeCell`
   stamps the family from the colour index on every decode (idempotent) instead of
   re-encoding megabytes of baked payloads.
4. **Nebula billboards / object gas (0.3.2)** — placed at generation, moved per frame
   through the `pattern` family. Deviation, deliberate: the rigid pattern rotation
   runs **in the nebula vertex shader** (same θ = ω_p·T about the model centre, T = 0
   identity) rather than through a per-quad CPU upload via `orbit.js` — a few hundred
   quads either way, but the GPU path keeps the frame loop allocation-free and shares
   the star law's `camera.dynA.z`. Object *members* carry their family in the record:
   age < 100 Myr → pattern, old members (globulars) → their component's family.
5. **Path C density quads (future)** — spec-level requirement recorded here: cells
   rotate with their dominant-component family so the glow follows the points.

The camera never moves by itself in this feature (it is the user's). One frame detail
worth spelling out: in the Milky Way preset the sun-centred origin is just where
`T = 0` puts the Sun — the Sun itself is a `disc`-family body and orbits the model
centre like everything else, drifting away from the origin as T advances. At `T = 0`
the sky is exactly today's.

## Out of scope

- Gravity, N-body, live orbit integration of any kind. Orbits are closed-form, forever.
- Reverse time, per-star time, time per region. One global T.
- Stellar evolution under T (births/deaths as time advances — a possible follow-up;
  here luminosities are frozen, only positions move).
- Motion blur / temporal anti-aliasing for the high-rate strobe.
- Bar buckling, pattern-speed evolution, satellite galaxies on hyperbolic fly-bys
  (tempting with the `pressure` family in hand — later).
