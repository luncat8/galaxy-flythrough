# 0.4.8 — Realistic 3D shapes

## Goal

Every component of the density field must reproduce the *typical 3D shape* of the
structure it stands for, not only its face-on outline. The visible failure is the
bar: `PROFILE_BAR` puts a **constant density inside a boxy superellipsoid**, so a
`SB*` bar is literally a rectangular block in `z` — a flat top and bottom, a sharp
rim, a slab seen edge-on. Real bars are smooth, vertically extended
boxy/peanut structures with no edge anywhere.

The bar is milestone 1 because it is the only component with a *hard* density
step. The rest of the field is audited in the same pass (milestone 3) so the same
class of mistake cannot hide in the disc, spheroid or halo.

## 1. What the field does today (audit)

| component | field | 3D read | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| thin disc | `exp(-R/L)·sech²(z/2H)`, flare, soft core | smooth, truncated at 25 kpc / 3 kpc where the density is `e^-9.6` / `e^-20` | fine |
| thick disc | `exp(-R/L)·exp(-\|z\|/H)` | smooth; the `\|z\| = 3 kpc` cut leaves a 3.6% step of the *thick* disc, 0.4% of the local total | M3: soften or justify |
| spheroid (Plummer / Sérsic) | smooth, cut at `s ≤ 6` / `s ≤ 8` | Plummer step 3e-5 of peak; Sérsic n=4 at `s = 8` is 5e-3 | M3: re-check the Sérsic cut |
| **bar** | **uniform inside the peanut superellipsoid** | **flat top/bottom, hard rim, constant density in the cross-section** | **M1: rewrite** |
| halo | `r^-3.5` cut at 100 kpc | 1e-7 of the peak | fine |
| arms | in-plane modulation `1 + A·cos(mφ − K ln R)` | z-independent by design (a population fact, not a shape bug) | keep |

Measured on `SBa` (a=1.80, b=0.56, c=0.60 kpc, n=4, peanut 0.55): `ρ(0,0,z)` is
exactly `1.000` for every `|z| ≤ 0.60` kpc and exactly `0` past it — a
rectangular profile, and the same in `y`. That is what the eye reads as "not a
real galaxy".

## 2. Observed targets (numbers this release is tuned to)

* Bars are triaxial and boxy: CALIFA medians `B/A ≈ 0.31`, `C/A ≈ 0.34`; boxiness
  exponents in the 2.5–4 range. (Méndez-Abreu et al. 2018, MNRAS 479, 4172;
  Athanassoula et al. 1990; Sormani et al. 2022, MNRAS, potential–density pairs.)
* Along the bar's principal axes the density falls approximately exponentially
  with scale lengths `0.70 : 0.44 : 0.18` kpc — i.e. `h_z/L ≈ 0.26`, so the
  "extent" of an ellipsoid fit is roughly 3-4 vertical scale heights.
  (Wegg & Gerhard 2013, via Wegg 2014, "The Galactic Bar", arXiv:1408.0219.)
* The vertical profile of a bar is **not** flat-topped: `sech²` fits the N-body
  vertical structure better than an exponential (Wegg et al. 2015, MNRAS 450,
  4050), and the Milky Way's bulge map is completed with a `sech²` fit to every
  vertical slice (Portail et al. 2017, MNRAS 465, 1621).
* The vertical scale height is **not constant along the bar**: it rises from the
  centre to a peak near the end of the boxy/peanut region and falls again through
  the long bar (Wegg et al. 2015). In models this is the "peanut height function"
  `Z_bar(x)` — a double Gaussian at `±R_pea` over a floor (Fragkoudi et al. 2015;
  Tahmasebzadeh et al. 2023, MNRAS, arXiv:2309.11557).
* The Milky Way's B/P bulge shows a strong X-shape above ≈ 400 pc and is about as
  strong as NGC 128's (Wegg & Gerhard 2013; ESO Messenger 154). Reference image
  used for the visual check: NGC 4710 (HST `heic0914b`) — smooth, tapered,
  vertically extended, no edge anywhere.

## 3. M1 — the bar profile

### 3.1 Field

Bar frame as today: `ξ = x_b/(a·r0)`, `η = y_b/(b·r0)`, `ζ = z_b/(c·r0)`, tilt
`tiltDeg`, in-plane boxiness `n` (`spheroid.n`), `tip = min(1, spheroidRadius)`.

```
τ(ξ)  = (tipⁿ − |ξ|ⁿ)^(1/n)                    cross-section radius (unchanged)
P(ξ)  = 1 + peanut·ξ²                          peanut height factor (unchanged)
L(ξ)  = plateau, then exp(−(ξ−plateau)/endCap) longitudinal profile (unchanged)

u = η / τ(ξ)                                   slice coordinates
v = ζ / (P(ξ)·τ(ξ))

ρ_bar = amp · L(ξ) · (1 − |u|ⁿ − |v|^cᵥ)^q     for |u|ⁿ + |v|^cᵥ ≤ 1, else 0
```

with

* `n` — in-plane boxiness (existing anchor `BAR_BOXINESS`, 4 … 2.5),
* `cᵥ` — **new**: the vertical profile exponent (`bar.vertical`, anchor
  `BAR_VERTICAL`). Over 2 the support is a smooth-topped superellipse and the
  midplane is flat; under 2 the profile turns over into a cusp at the midplane
  (ρ(z) ∝ 1 − |z/z_h|^cᵥ has infinite slope at 0), which reads as a crease
  edge-on. The first draft's 1.8 → 1.3 was therefore rejected on measurement and
  the shipped anchors are **2.60 → 2.00** (`[[-1,2.60],[1,2.60],[3,2.40],
  [5,2.20],[6,2.00],[9,2.00]]`): squarer than an ellipse near the centre where the
  peanut lives, relaxing to a round-ish support in the late types. `BAR_NONE`
  carries 2.0 for the models that never read it,
* `q = 1.5` — the interior falloff constant (`BAR_SLICE_FALLOFF`). It vanishes
  with zero slope at the envelope (`q ≥ 1`), which is what removes the rim.

`ρ` is bounded and smooth everywhere: it reaches exactly 0 at the envelope with
zero gradient, so no cut is ever visible — the same property the Plummer bulge
has at its truncation.

### 3.2 Why this family

* It **generalises what is there**: `q = 0` reproduces "uniform in the unit Lⁿ
  disk" exactly, and the slice mass identity below shows `lnDiskArea(n)` is the
  `q = 0` case, so the change is a strict refinement of the old field.
* It **keeps the longitudinal machinery**: the slice mass is independent of `ξ`
  (the slice is a fixed shape scaled by `τ` and `P·τ`), so the mass per slab is
  still `L(ξ)·P(ξ)·τ(ξ)²` — `barLongitudinalWeight`, the `ξ` CDF, `tip`, the
  truncation fractions and the arm-couples-to-the-bar-end rule do not move.
* The knobs are observed properties: `n` = face-on boxiness, `cᵥ` = vertical
  profile thinness, `q` = interior flatness. No new mechanism.
* It is **exactly invertible in one pass** (§3.4), so the sampler stays
  rejection-free.

### 3.3 Slice mass (closed form)

```
m_slice(n, cᵥ, q) = ∫∫ (1 − |u|ⁿ − |v|^cᵥ)^q du dv
                  = 4 · K(cᵥ,q) · (1/n) · B(1/n, q + 1/cᵥ + 1)
K(c,q)            = (1/c) · B(1/c, q+1)
```

with `B` the Beta function, evaluated through `density.logGamma` (already in the
file — `lnDiskArea` is the same machinery). `massIntegrals.bulge` keeps its
structure and only swaps `lnDiskArea(n)` for `m_slice(n, cᵥ, q)`:

```
massIntegrals.bulge = 2 · a·b·c·r0³ · m_slice · barLongitudinalIntegral(model, 1)
```

Checks this buys, all analytic: `q = 0, cᵥ = n` ⇒ `m_slice = lnDiskArea(n)`
(exact identity, pinned by test); `n = 2, cᵥ = 2, q = 1` ⇒
`m_slice = π/2` and the total is `(2/3)·π·a·b·c·r0³`.

### 3.4 Sampler (exact, no rejection)

The slice is self-similar, so both draws are one-dimensional and scale-free:

* `ξ` — unchanged 1024-bin inverse CDF on `barLongitudinalWeight`.
* `u` — marginal of the slice at fixed `ξ`:
  `p(u) ∝ (1 − |u|ⁿ)^(q + 1/cᵥ)`, `|u| ≤ 1` (the `v` integral contributes
  `(1−|u|ⁿ)^q · (1−|u|ⁿ)^(1/cᵥ) · K`).
* `v` — conditional: `v = V·w`, `V = (1−|u|ⁿ)^(1/cᵥ)`, `w ∈ [0,1]` with
  `p(w) ∝ (1 − w^cᵥ)^q` — **independent of `u`**, so one fixed table serves every
  slice.

Both tables are built once per model (Simpson, the same 1024-bin contract as the
disc radial CDF). The two signs come from two more hash draws of the star seed.
Output is unchanged: `η = b·r0·τ·u`, `ζ = c·r0·P·τ·v`.

### 3.5 Uniform layout

`barShape` = `(peanut, endCap, plateau, vertical)` — the fourth lane was unused,
so the WGSL struct, the packer layout and `DENSITY_PARAMS_FLOATS` do not grow.
`BAR_NONE` carries `vertical: 2` for the models that never read it.

### 3.6 Tests

* `galaxy-types-test.js`: replace the "n=2 bar is exactly the ellipsoid volume"
  pin with the new analytic pin `(2/3)π·abc·r0³`, and re-derive `barLevel` (the
  body is the slice envelope, not the `s ≤ 1` superellipsoid) for the crop test.
  Keep the delivered-mass identity, the 0.02 mix tolerance and the
  nothing-outside-the-truncation checks.
* `model-parity-test.js`: the bar's vertical marginal expectation becomes the
  integral of the new slice profile over `ξ` (numeric, built from
  `density.barSliceProfile`); the peanut check is re-stated on a robust pair of
  probes (a `z` that is inside the lobe and outside the centre).
* `density-distribution-test.js`: the SBa box histogram must still match the
  field (the tolerance stays 8% total variation).
* New `experiments/shape-test.js`: the *shape* gate this release exists for —
  monotone `|z|` decay, no step larger than 2% of the local peak across any
  slice, the peanut (lobes thicker than the centre), the half-density height in
  the observed band, the analytic slice mass against a numeric integral, and
  sampler-vs-field parity for `ξ`, `R` and `z`.
* `wgsl-validate` pins the struct/layout automatically once `barShape.w` is named.

## 4. M2 — the Milky Way preset's bulge

Today the preset's central component is a triaxial Plummer ellipsoid
(`a 1.5, b 0.5, c 0.4, r0 1, amp 12, tilt 27°`). It is smooth, but it is a
*spheroid*, and the real Milky Way's bulge is a boxy/peanut bar. The bar profile
now exists, so the preset should use it — with three constraints:

1. the central density stays the thin disc's peak × ~12 (the authored contrast),
2. the bulge's *mass* stays close to today's (`massIntegrals` of the Plummer,
   which is dominated by the tail out to `s = 6`; the bounded bar has to be
   re-authored — longer, or a higher `amp` — to match),
3. the arms keep leaving at the bar's end (`arms.minRadius = spheroid.a·r0`).

Open authoring questions for the milestone, to be settled with numbers:
bar half-length (the B/P bulge is ≈ 2 kpc, the long bar ≈ 4-5 kpc), `b` and `c`
from the observed `B/A`, `C/A`, and whether the preset represents the *bulge*
(the inner B/P) or the *whole bar including the long bar*.

## 5. M3 — the sweep

* Sérsic/E truncation: `s ≤ 8` costs 5e-3 of the peak for `n = 4` — check the
  rendered edge and either move the cut or soften it.
* Disc truncation `|z| ≤ discHeight`: 0.4% step of the local total for the Milky
  Way preset; same treatment.
* `R ≤ discRadius`: invisible (`e^-9.6`), keep.
* Young/thin vertical structure: the model's young stars are already a separate
  population; check that its `z` distribution and the arm modulation do not need
  a vertical term of their own.
* Barlens: a third, inner boxy lens is a *population* structure (CALIFA barlenses
  are the face-on counterpart of the X-shape); out of scope unless asked.

## 8. Limits

* The peanut is still a *height function* `P(ξ) = 1 + peanut·ξ²` — the single
  parameter proxy for the literature's double-Gaussian `Z_bar(x)`. It makes the
  envelope thicker toward the bar's ends; it does not reproduce a dimpled X
  (concave) contour. A second vertical component is the honest way to get an X.
* The bar stays a bounded body (its own tip), unlike the disc's and halo's
  far-tail cuts. That is deliberate: a bar's end is a real feature of the shape.
* Star movement is untouched: orbits read the model's geometry, not the density
  profile, so the engines and their tests are unaffected.
