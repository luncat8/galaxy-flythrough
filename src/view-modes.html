			Drag looks around (view sphere) or grabs and pans the
			chart under the pointer · wheel zooms FoV / span ·
			Shift+wheel dollies the eye · Shift+drag
			slides it across the view.
			

			One heightmap, two layouts: the field is a function of direction,
			so the map and the sky cannot drift apart — the white dots are the stars
			the app draws, placed by the same mapping that sampled the field
			(experiments/view-modes-field-test.js pins it). Heights are
			Shepard IDW of log distance: smooth, hole-free, no Voronoi cells, no
			diffusion iterations.
			

			The colour range is per mode: auto follows the field the mode produced
			(mode 1 its own log-distance span, modes 2–3 the 5th–95th percentile),
			log when the raw spans decades; untick auto and the two sliders
			take over.
			The gradient is a real slope (dex of distance per degree of sky) with no
			hard top, so a steep ring keeps its shape instead of clumping at full
			scale.
			

			Relief lights that terrain: a north-west light at 45° shades the
			displayed height's own slope, so a ridge reads as a ridge. It multiplies
			the colour by at most 1 — a highlight that clipped would make the colour
			stop meaning the value the legend quotes — and at 0 the map is the flat
			ramp. The exaggeration is per degree of sky, so it does not change when
			the resolution does. Ramp picks the colours: auto keeps
			the ramp each mode was designed for, anything else overrides it for
			every mode.
			

			Every mode is a physical scalar, so a game can read it directly:
			ViewModesField.heightAt(field, layout, u, v, out) is a
			bilinear lookup of the last frame's grid.
