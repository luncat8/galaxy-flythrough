## 0.6.x — 2026-10-14 — 0.6.0 the epoch tier (E1) + the runtime coupling (E1b)

Direction for the session came from the user: *plan to improve galaxy evolution over
time*, scope *go straight for the first shippable slice* — so the plan artifact and
the first milestone shipped together, with no separate plan-review round.

**What the slice is.** The type table describes present-day galaxies: every anchor is
an observed value at `AGE_REF` (13.5 Gyr). 0.3.3 already re-solved the *population*
clock (`solvePopulationClock`: the SFH, every star's age, `gasNow`, the object mix)
but not the shape — a 2 Gyr Sb was today's Sb whose stars were young. 0.6.0 adds an
**epoch tier** beside it, solved from the same three numbers (`T`, `tauSfh`, `age`)
and the same `FORMATION_WINDOW` table, and exactly the reference epoch at `AGE_REF`:
each factor is a ratio of the same expression evaluated at the same point, so it is
`1` in IEEE arithmetic, multiplying by exactly 1 is exact, and an `AGE_REF` model is
bit-identical to the pre-0.6 model. That is what let every literal pin covering
today's galaxies stand untouched.

**The clock** (`src/math/galaxy.js`): `sfhMoment1(x) = 2 − e^−x(x² + 2x + 2)`, and

```
spheroidProgress = [F(min(age, 0.45·span_ref)/τ) − F(0)]
                 / [F(0.45·span_ref/τ) − F(0)]           span_ref = sfhSpanAt(AGE_REF, quench)
meanAge          = age − τ·M1(x)/F(x)                     x = x(age)
heat             = (meanAge / meanAge_ref)^HEAT_EXP       HEAT_EXP = 0.25
gasRatio         = gasFraction > 0 ? gasNow / gasFraction : 1
armContrast      = min(sqrt(gasRatio), ARM_CONTRAST_MAX)  ARM_CONTRAST_MAX = 2
```

`solveEpoch(populations, T)` clamps the clock once (one place, so the population and
structure tiers cannot hold different clocks) and `applyEpoch(model, epoch)` writes
the scaled groups, cloning every group it writes so a re-solve never mutates a group
another model object still shares. `model.epochBase` stores the reference values of
the eight scaled numbers (`thinH`, `thickH`, `thinAmp`, `thickAmp`, `spheroidAmp`,
`armsAmp`, `sigmaThin`, `sigmaThick`) for an exact undo.

**The law that shipped** scales three things and nothing else: the spheroid's
amplitude by `spheroidProgress` (the bulge/bar assembling — its length, boxiness and
pattern speed stay the type table's), the arms' amplitude by `armContrast` (arms are
the gas), and the disc's dispersion by `heat` with both scale heights by `heat²` (an
age–velocity relation with the isothermal `σ²` consequence). No length, angle or
profile exponent moves anywhere, so `omegaPattern`, `loopRatio`, `arms.minRadius` and
`arms.phase0` — and the orbit engines, the renderer's packed uniforms and the arm
ridge coupling that read them — are untouched at every epoch. The generated table
(`/tmp/epoch-table.js`, refs SBb thin.H 0.3 / armsAmp 0.2 / sphAmp 31.36, Sc
0.2548/0.25/0.7513, E4 0.585/0/0.1926): SBb 2 Gyr asm .08 heat .57 armC 2.00 sphP
.18 sphAmp 5.64 → 5 Gyr .35/.73/2.00/.77/24.17 → 8 Gyr .63/.84/1.76/1.00/31.36;
thin.H 0.024–0.047 at 0.2 Gyr; heat 0.27–1.00; monotone in age for every type,
exactly 1 at the reference epoch.

**Two candidate laws were measured and rejected** (they are recorded in the plan so a
fork cannot re-adopt them by accident):

* *Windows against the epoch's own compressed span.* Compressed windows are
  self-similar, so the ratios only scale the galaxy's total and then drift
  non-physically: an S0's thick disc and spheroid ran ahead and saturated by 1 Gyr
  while the thin disc lagged. The reference-span schedule `min(age, w·span_ref)` is
  the shipped form.
* *Every component's amplitude scaled by its own window.* Green for `galaxy-types`
  and wrong: a 0.5 Gyr field emptied its thin and thick disc to 0.6 % of present mass
  while the gas-driven objects (nebulae, HII regions, open clusters — they scale with
  `gasNow`) stayed full, and globular counts *rose* with age (SBb 302 → 88). The field
  contradicted its own gas layer. The disc and halo therefore keep their present mass
  at every epoch; the mass budget moves with the gas field that can follow it (E3).

**One coupling the first pass missed, caught by a pin.** With heat applied
unconditionally, a 0.5 Gyr E4 (mid-burst, `gasNow` 0.2, gas-rich) stopped placing its
six open clusters, because the E sequence has `thin.amp = thick.amp = 0` — no disc at
all — yet its two heights are read by the gas lane, and an 8× thinner lane collapsed
`inDisc` to nothing. The old test row had it right and the model had the bug, so the
fix went into `applyEpoch`, not the pin: the disc heat is now gated on the model
*having* a disc (`thin.amp > 0 ∥ thick.amp > 0`, one comparison, no per-type
constant). A model with no disc keeps its heights and dispersions; a starburst keeps
its HII regions.

**Runtime coupling (E1b).** The epoch is packed geometry, so `sameGeometry` now fails
across an age change and `renderer.regenerate` takes the sampling path: an age step
re-draws the field. Two pieces of runtime state were seeded from the old birth
positions and had to follow:

* `main.js` `regenerateAge` calls `initSimpleLandmarks()` (the CPU mirror re-seeds
  from birth, exactly as a type change does — prepare re-seeded the GPU thetas).
* The selection set is re-resolved by key (`main.js` `resolveSet()`): the keys are
  `fieldStarSeed(seed, slot)`, a function of the seed and the draw index, so a
  re-sample keeps every key valid while positions, colours, magnitudes and stellar
  states all move. One pass over the live blocks fills an open-addressed map of the
  set's keys, then every hit rewrites slot, block kind, position, colour, magnitude
  and the five stellar inputs from the record the key now names; a key that is not
  resident (or not visible) keeps its readout line and loses its slot, which is the
  ring's existing missing-slot behaviour. `star-set.js` lost the two unused 0.5.0
  helpers that described the old contract (`refreshStellar`, `refreshSlots`) and its
  header now describes the shipped one. The label layer's key→slot map already
  rebuilds when `dynamicVersion` moves, which a re-sample does, so the rings follow
  the field with no change there.

**Pins re-stated where the contract genuinely changed** (never the other way round):

* `galaxy-types` — the `sameGeometry` age pin became the epoch-is-structure contract
  (green at 513/513): true for a preset/table re-derived at `AGE_REF` and for a gas
  override at the reference epoch; false for another age, an age override, a
  `tauSfh` override, a same-young-epoch pair under two SFH timescales, thin-H,
  arms-amp, another seed, another type.
* `renderer` — the age section now pins a re-draw (fieldSamples +1), surviving
  identity keys, a resolved key following the new field (positions and records move),
  the unchanged landmark block, and the exposure offset's sign at 0.5 Gyr
  (125/125: the age section above plus one new check that the population-only
  reuse path is still live for a gas override at the reference epoch).
* `galaxy-age` — the exposure section samples the field from the epoch's own model,
  the way the renderer does (`sampleField`); one field used at every age would be
  measuring the tier's structural term instead of the population's. E4's gas-driven
  row keeps its original fall claim (see the heat gate above). 40/40.
* `m1-smoke` — a static pin that the age path re-seeds the mirror and calls
  `resolveSet` (61/61).
* `galaxy-evolution` — new, 18 checks: reference exactness in both directions, the
  factors' monotonicity and bounds for all 19 types, only-the-four-amplitudes-move,
  field honesty (the sampled arms carry the parameter's contrast: mean azimuthal
  cosine ≈ `arms.amp/2` on 300 k stars, the young/reference ratio matching
  `armContrast`; the sampled bulge share matching `deliveredMasses`), and the disc
  law.
