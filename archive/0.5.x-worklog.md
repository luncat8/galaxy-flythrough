Two architectural rules are new and are stated as such in §1: planets are **CPU-only** (no
WGSL, no `StarPacked` growth, drawn on a 2D canvas overlay like `label-layer.js`), and
generation is **lazy and never resident** (a system is a pure function of a key, derived on
demand, no cache that grows). The consequence written into the acceptance criteria is that
`wgsl-validate`, `wgsl-exec` and `renderer-test` must not change during this series.

The load-bearing decision is star identity (§2). Selection cannot be a buffer index: the
renderer already documents that *"streaming slots are not stable star identities"*, because
the local gap-fill and catalog blocks are rewritten on every residency change. So every
drawn star gets a stable `u32` key in a parallel `identity` array, and a selection stores a
*snapshot* (key, position, stellar inputs) rather than an index. Stellar inputs come from
the same `deriveStar` call the renderer used for the three procedural blocks (bit-exact) and
from a new `massFromClassAndMag` inversion for landmarks and catalog stars, whose 16-byte
records carry only colour index and absolute magnitude.

Every number the plan pins was computed with the plan's own formulas before being written
down, not quoted from memory — that is what caught five errors in the first draft:

- the Hohmann launch phase angle is `π·(1 − 2·t_H/P2)`, not `π·(1 − t_H/P2)`; the corrected
  form gives 44.4° for Earth→Mars against the published 44°.
- the brachistochrone distance is the half-ellipse arc `π·(a1+a2)/2`, not the radius gap
  `|a2 − a1|`. With the gap, `dv_x` for Earth→Mars came out at 14 km/s — inside a chemical
  tank — and a 1 g ship read 2.1 days. With the arc, `dv_x = 106 km/s`, a chemical hull
  cannot beat the ellipse at all (258.8 d whatever the budget), fusion gives 32.5 d and a
  1 g hull 5.6 d.
- the surface-temperature model needed a mass gate. One greenhouse table made every
  habitable-band world Earth-like, including Mars. With `AIRLESS_M = 0.02 M⊕` and
  `ATM_RETAIN_M = 0.3 M⊕` gating the display type, plus a carbonate-silicate thermostat
  above the gate and a runaway branch (ΔT 500 K, albedo 0.70) above the HZ inner edge, six
  real bodies reproduce from four constants: Mercury 433/440 K, Venus 742/737, Earth
  288/288, Mars 216/210, Moon 270/250, Ceres 163/168.
- ship classes had to satisfy `dv ≥ 2·v_cruise` or the table lies about reaching its own
  cruise speed; fusion and generation were both inconsistent as first drafted.
- and the generation row of the transit table was computed from 0.01 m/s² while §8.1 says
  0.001, which hid a property worth stating: at 0.001 m/s² its ceiling on Earth→Mars is
  48.7 km/s, below the 106 km/s crossover, so a 6 000 km/s tank buys **no** in-system
  speed-up over a 20 km/s chemical hull (258.8 d either way). Correct for the class — it is
  an endurance vehicle whose tank takes 190 years to burn — but only visible if the numbers
  are recomputed rather than reused.

The delta-v module reproduces the published Solar-System map on 19 pins (surface→LEO 9.66
against 9.4, LEO→GEO 3.91 against 3.9, C3=0 escape 3.21 against 3.2, LEO→low Mars orbit
5.04 against ≈5.0, Earth→Mars 258.8 d against 259, surface→surface 15.37 against 15–18),
and two structural invariants on the same real bodies: Earth has a GEO node
(42 163 km < 0.4·r_Hill) and the Moon does not (88 434 km > 24 605 km).

Known limits are recorded in plan §12 rather than left implicit: the HZ inherits
`luminosityFromMass`'s late-M over-luminosity (a TRAPPIST-1 analog's HZ sits 2.7× too far
out — an existing population law, not a new error, and changing it is 0.3/0.4 territory);
the gas-giant mass–radius law is radius-saturating, so Saturn comes out 15% too large;
transfers are coplanar, circular and assist-free; the brachistochrone branch ignores the
destination's orbital velocity and is gated behind `dv_x` for that reason.

## 0.5.0 — 2026-10-12 — Star selection implemented and pinned

0.5.0 is done: a star can be picked out of the five record blocks, kept in a bounded
selection set, flown to, and the neighbourhood swept by world-space radius. Nothing above
the renderer's existing accessors moved — the star buffer, the WGSL and the three render
paths are untouched, exactly as §1.1 requires.

**Identity keys (§2.2).** Every drawn record gets a `u32` key in a parallel `identity`
array, written by the block's own writer where the key's inputs are already in hand:
`fieldStarSeed` for the procedural block, `hash4(DOMAIN_LANDMARK, i, 0, 0)` for the named
table, the member seed for composite objects, the slot seed for catalog gap-fill stars and
`hash4(DOMAIN_CATALOG, cellId, slot, 0)` for streaming slots. Streaming slots are exactly
why a selection cannot be a buffer index — the local and catalog blocks are rewritten on
every residency change, so a snapshot (key, distance) is the only stable handle.

**The set and the pick.** `core/star-set.js` is the 256-member snapshot set, preallocated,
nearest-first insert, primary at slot 0, capacity evicting the farthest. `selection.js`
gained `pickAny` — a walk over all five blocks through `blocks.float32`/`blocks.uint32`
(typed arrays, not DataView: a 300k pass with method calls per field costs more than the
dispatch it feeds), a 3 px tie band where brightness wins and distance wins outside it, and
a `time === 0` fast path that skips the engine dispatch entirely.

**Two deviations from the plan, recorded in §11.1.** `star-set.js` loads *before*
`selection.js` (`createSelection` reads `STAR_SET_CAPACITY` to size the radius result), and
§3.6's readout lives in a dedicated `#selection` `<pre>` rather than in more `#overlay`
lines, because `selrad` and `selstars` are serialized preset state and the readout and the
preset have to agree.

**The measurement, and what it found.** `experiments/star-select-test.js` (63 checks,
wired into `run.py` as `star-select`) pins: pick parity for masks 1/4/5 across all five
blocks (the picked record's projected position equals an independent dispatch of its own
bytes within 0.5 px), the simple mask reporting `recordPositionApproximated()` for
2/3/6/7, the apocenter subset partition at share 0.5, the tie-break both sides of the band,
misses (no source, empty, all-invisible, camera facing away — no throw), select-around
against a brute-force list with capacity truncation at 300k, the star-set contract,
key→slot round-trips, `flyToStar` stand-offs (Sun 0.0006 kpc, supergiant clamped to 0.01,
dwarf clamped to the orbit floor) and the two click budgets.

Timings (`experiments/logs/star-select.json`): the frozen-sky pick walks 300k records in
**3.2 ms**, inside the 16 ms budget. The *dispatched* pick costs **55 ms** — over a frame,
one `orbitPosition` per record. The plan's own remedy (§3.1) is the view-axis pre-reject,
which is not built; the limit is recorded in the 0.5 plan §12 and `plan.md` §12 with the
measurement beside it, and open question 1 of the plan is now answered "pre-reject, not a
spatial index" — the buffer is rewritten per frame, so an index would be rebuilt as often
as it is queried. The radius scan is inside budget at every radius from 50 ly to
100 000 ly, because its cost follows the number of matches and saturates at the 256-member
capacity.

**Two defects the test caught, both fixed here.**

- `label-layer.js`'s key→slot map read `mapSlots[h]` without a "not found" sentinel, so a
  set key that is not resident resolved to slot 0 and rang whichever star happened to sit
  there — a departed star could keep a ring, and a wrong one could appear. One
  `mapSlots.fill(-1)` fixes it; the test now pins that a non-resident key draws nothing.
- `selectAround`'s insertion sort walked the whole 256-slot array for every match, so a
  3 kpc radius (≈80k matches at 300k) cost 18 ms. A full array can only reject a newcomer
  no nearer than the farthest it already holds, so that case now short-circuits: provably
  the same answer, ~2 ms at any radius.

**Validation.** `python3 scripts/run.py all-tests` → ALL TESTS PASSED, before and after
the two fixes. The run rewrote the `date` field of 19 log files (plus machine-dependent
timings in five of them); those are reverted so the diff carries only the three logs with
real new content (`preset`, `export-parity`, `m1-smoke`) and the new `star-select.json`.

and channel map — since every other 0.5.1 task (occurrence, architecture, insolation,

## 0.5.1 — 2026-10-12 — The lazy planet generator shipped

`src/math/planets.js` (~890 lines) implements the whole 0.5.1 release in one module, plus
`experiments/planet-gen-test.js` (73 checks, all passing) and `experiments/logs/planet-gen.json`.
No renderer, no WGSL, no `StarPacked` growth — the two ground rules §1 states, kept: the
module has no import of anything in `src/render/`, and `wgsl-validate`, `wgsl-exec` and
`renderer-test` are untouched by this release.

**Shape.** One hash tier and one record tier. Every system is `hash4(key, model.seed,
DOMAIN_PLANETS, blockKind)` and then ~120 `hash01At(systemSeed, channel)` draws against
the §2.3 channel map, so a system is a pure function of a key and a galaxy: re-rolled with
a different `model.seed`, it is unrelated, and byte-identical on a second call. The output
is a struct-of-arrays `SystemRecord` (~1.5 KB) with a *summary tier* that is the same walk
with the orbit draws skipped, which is what makes the search's first pass affordable
(1.1 µs median against the 6 µs budget) and the summary identity an exact invariant rather
than a coincidence.

**Stellar inputs are per-block, and the three procedural blocks are bit-exact.** The
procedural field's key *is* `fieldStarSeed(seed, i)`, so `i` falls out of the key by
subtraction and `deriveStar` runs again with the same arguments the renderer used — no
scan, no approximation. The local gap-fill block's key is the slot seed, so the renderer's
own `deriveSeed = imul(slotSeed, 31) + 5` falls out of it too. Object members replay the
placement (`placeObjects` is deterministic) and derive at the object's coeval age; that
needed two new additive exports in `objects.js` (`objectMemberSeed`, and
`objectMemberComponent`, which `writeObjectMembers` now calls instead of inlining the same
four-line switch — one rule, one definition). Landmarks and catalog records keep only a
colour index and an absolute magnitude, so they take the §2.5 inversion path.

**The inversion rule that produced the three mass pins.** L from M_V; the class bracket
picks the power-law branch its *lower* bound sits in; closed-form inverse, clamped to the
bracket. A class whose bracket cannot reach the inverted luminosity at all — a late K star
dimmer than 0.55 M☉ allows — is answered with the bracket's *midpoint*, not the clamp.
That is the 61 Cyg case (0.70 M☉), and it is why the K pin is a number rather than a wall.
Measured: Sun 1.000, Sirius A 2.19 (pin 2.0 ± 0.2), a K landmark 0.700.

**The one measurement that changed the code.** The first full run put `systemSummary` at
6.6 µs against a 6 µs budget while the same call in a two-module harness ran at 2.0 µs. A
CPU profile showed the difference was not the model: `internalModuleStat` — the filesystem
stats — dominated, because `planets.js`'s `mod(windowName, requirePath)` helper resolves a
dependency *per call*, and the generator calls it twice per system. Under Node that is a
`require()` per call. Memoizing the five lookups (`hashDep`/`densityDep`/… resolved once on
first use, which is also the page's behaviour — `window.HashLib` is a property read either
way) took the summary to 1.08 µs and `generateSystem` to 1.56 µs. Recorded in
`findings-pitfalls-skills.md`; the plan's §4.9/§4.10 budgets now carry the measured figures
beside them.

**Deviations, all recorded in §11.1 of the plan** so a fork reimplementing from the plan
matches what exists: the moon count got its own channel (40 + 8·p) rather than sharing the
rotation row; belt stipple seeds are the raw word of channel 204/214 and the dots are
hashed on the belt's own seed; mass inside a bulk range is log-uniform; the third density
pin is `5 M⊕ → 6730 kg/m³` with the cross-check restated as the law putting a 1.13 M⊕
super-earth 3 % off α Cen Bb; and §4.5's "three giants cannot fit inside 40 AU" aside is
wrong (a log-uniform draw over the 1430× span already spaces neighbours ~1.9× while
K_HILL needs 1.5×), so the invariant test pins the mechanism instead of the anecdote.

**Validation.** `python3 scripts/run.py all-tests` → ALL TESTS PASSED. `m1-smoke-test.js`
passes with `planets.js` in the page exactly once, between `star-types.js` and `orbit.js`.
`objects.js` gained two exports and one call-site refactor (`objectMemberComponent`), and
`object-placement-test.js` still passes unchanged, which is the refactor check.

Next step: 0.5.2-1, `core/search.js` — the two-stage scan (filter on the record's
temperature field first, then `systemSummary` on survivors). The generator's summary tier
was built for exactly that stage, and its 1.1 µs is what makes a 300k-star scan land
inside the plan's ~1.8 s worst case.

**Post-ship correction (same day): the moon-count channel.** The first full run passed 73/73,
and re-deriving the §2.3 channel map by hand found a collision the tests did not cover: the
moon count was drawn at `40 + 8·p`, and `40 + 8·0` is also planet 1's bulk-class channel
(`32 + 8·1 = 40`). Planet 0's moon count and planet 1's bulk class were therefore the same
draw — a channel map's worst failure mode, and one the shipped checks could not see because
nothing compares planet 0's moons against planet 1's bulk. The fix moves moon counts to
`128 + p`, one channel each in the gap twelve 8-wide planet slots leave at 128–199, and adds
two pins: one that claims every channel in the map into one `Set` and fails on the first
duplicate, and one that asserts the moon range sits outside the planet block and inside the
spare gap. `planet-gen-test.js` is now 75 checks. The plan's §2.3 table and §11.1 deviation
record both carry the corrected layout.

The generalisable rule, appended to `findings-pitfalls-skills.md`: **a channel map is a
claimed address space, so the test is an occupancy set, not a spot-check.** Pinning
"channel 32 gives the bulk class" passes while channels 40 and 40 both being the bulk class
of neighbouring planets goes unnoticed. When a plan writes down a map of channel → quantity,
the implementing test should register every (channel, consumer) pair in one structure and
assert no channel appears twice — which is also what catches the next collision for free.

**Second post-ship correction (same day): reused records kept a stale terran candidate.**
The four `bestTerran*` scalars were written only when a candidate was found, so a summary
record reused across calls reported the *previous* system's best candidate after a barren
one. `walk` now zeroes them with the histograms, and the test replaces the single-shot
check with a real reuse test: it runs 2 000 keys through one record, counts the systems
that seed a candidate, and requires the next barren ones to report zeros two times over.
`planet-gen-test.js` is now 75 checks (the channel-map pair from the first correction plus
this one), and the plan's §4.9 field list states the reset.

## 0.5.2 — 2026-10-12 — Star and planet search implemented and pinned

`src/core/search.js` (one accessor in, result buffers out) plus the menu block, the two
preset fields and `experiments/search-test.js` (27 checks) with
`experiments/logs/search.json`. No renderer, WGSL or record change again — the scan reads
the 16-byte `StarPacked` word and the `identity` array the 0.5.0 accessor already exposes.

**Two stages, in the plan's order.** Stage 1 answers a class, state, luminosity or distance
query from the record's own bytes — colour index, magnitude (luminosity bounds folded into
magnitude bounds so no star takes a `log10`), position — and takes one `stellarInputs`
derivation per star only when the filter asks for a mass or a component. Stage 2 runs
`systemSummary` on the survivors, with the stage-1 snapshot passed in rather than derived
twice. A count-only planet query names no planet, so a `terran` scan reports the star's
best-terran candidate and the rest report nothing.

**The summary tier gained an `env` block** — `a`, `mass`, `teq`, `tsurf`, `display` per
planet, bounded by `envCount` — because §5.1's range predicates (`tsurfMin/Max`,
`teqMin/Max`, `planetMassMin/Max`, `aMin/Max`) are per-planet predicates and the shipped
summary tier kept only counts. The walk already classifies every planet before the orbit
side is skipped, so the block is the same values the full record writes, in both tiers,
never re-derived. Recorded in §11.1 and in §4.9/§4.10's field lists.

**Measured numbers, all from `logs/search.json`** (SBb, seed 3, 300 000 procedural stars):

| query | measured | budget |
|---|---|---|
| class filter, galaxy scope | **1.2 ms** (stage 2: 0.0 ms) | < 120 ms — inside |
| mass filter, galaxy scope | 110 ms — one `deriveStar` per star | inside, 90× the class filter |
| planet filter (`terran`), galaxy scope | **248 ms** | < 600 ms — inside |
| planet filter, 214-star selection scope | **1.5 ms** | < 2 ms — inside |

All four are inside, so 0.5.2-5 (the frame-chunked `search.step()`) is **not built** — the
plan's own rule, measure then decide. The mass filter is the one case it was designed for:
110 ms of the 120 ms budget, because the record does not carry a mass.

**Scope, and saying which one ran.** `auto` (the default) resolves to the selection whenever
the set holds anything and to the galaxy otherwise; the two explicit scopes are selectable,
and `stats.scope` reports the scope that *ran*, printed in `#sysread`'s header beside the
scanned/matched counts, whether the list was cut, and the stage time. `scanned` counts the
records the filter actually saw: the set's resident members under `selection` — a key that
is not resident right now is never scanned at all — and every resident record under
`galaxy`. The membership test is a 1024-slot open-addressing table rebuilt from the set's
≤256 keys per scan, so the selection scope is one pass over the records rather than 256 of
them.

**Deviations, recorded in §11.1**: the `env` block above; the preset token is
`cls=G,K;pt=terran;ts=220-300` segments rather than §5.5's positional `G,K|terran|220-300`
(a positional token breaks the moment a viewer sets a later field and skips an earlier
one); the tri-state scope; and `stats.scope` reporting the resolved scope.

**The parity suite is the test's core.** Every predicate in the §5.1 table — 27 filter
combinations across classes, states, luminosity, mass, components, distance, planet types,
Tsurf, Teq, planet mass, semi-major axis, min-planets, belt and barren — is evaluated twice:
once by the scan, once by an independent evaluator that runs the generator's **full**
record rather than its summary tier, over a fixture that is a real `sampleGalaxyStars` field
plus the object members written by `objects.js`'s own writer. That full-record evaluator is
also what pins the new `env` block (every planet of 300 systems, field for field) and what
proves the summary walk answers a range filter exactly as the full walk does. Writing it
caught a bug in the *test* first: the reference built refs without the record's class and
magnitude, so `recordInputs` fell back to the solar defaults and disagreed with the scan on
exactly the landmark, catalog and local records — the discrepancy was the harness's, and it
was found by looking at which records mismatched rather than by trusting either side.

**Validation.** `python3 scripts/run.py all-tests` → ALL TESTS PASSED, with `search` wired
into `TYPES['test']` and `m1-smoke-test.js`'s namespace list (the page scope check also

## 0.5.3.1 — 2026-10-12 — The system-view skeleton: canvas, readout, enter/exit

`src/render/system-layer.js` (the `#system` 2D overlay, opened and closed, nothing drawn
into it yet), the menu's `System` block, the `V` / `Esc` keys, and the enter/exit plumbing
that generates the primary's system and turns `#sysread` into its readout. Still no
renderer, WGSL or record change: the layer is a canvas and a state machine, the system
itself is the 0.5.1 generator called once per entry.

**One module, one canvas, one boolean gate.** The layer follows `label-layer.js`'s
contract exactly — device-pixel backing store, CSS-pixel drawing under the
`devicePixelRatio` transform, `resize` fed the *WebGPU* canvas's client size (the
`#system` element is `display: none` while closed, and a hidden element has no client
size), clear-on-disable because a canvas keeps its last frame. `draw()` is one boolean
test and a return while the view is closed, so a closed view costs the frame loop nothing.
A caller with no 2D context gets an inert layer rather than a fatal one, which is what the
wiring tests hand it.

**The system is generated on entry, never cached.** `openSystemView` re-derives the
primary's system into one preallocated `SystemRecord` and one preallocated ref, exactly as
`core/search.js` derives its per-record snapshots: the ref is the record's own key, block
index, slot, stored position, `fieldBuffers()` and the packed colour index / absolute
magnitude, plus the landmark's own name when the slot is a named record (so a landmark's
readout designates it by name, everything else by the `S<key36>` form of §4.11). Nothing is
keyed or memoised — closing and reopening the same star reproduces byte-identical records,
which is the property the generator was built for, and a star that leaves the residency set
stops being describable rather than going stale.

**Enter/exit, per §6.5.** The `System view` button or `V` opens (a primary that is not
resident is a no-op — the view requires a describable star); the camera goes to orbit
object on the star, flying in on the `flyToStar` stand-off only when it is farther than a
parsec away, so a camera already at the star turns instead of jumping. `V` again, `Esc` or
the button closes: the layer erases the canvas, the element hides, the camera keeps its
orbit target, and the readout returns to whatever it held before. `Esc` is routed through
`input.js`'s press map like the other one-shot keys but is the one action that does *not*
`preventDefault` — the pointer lock releases on Escape in fly mode, and swallowing the
default there would trap the cursor.

**The view tracks the primary.** `N`, a plain click or a search-add that changes the
primary regenerates the system in place; `X`, `R` and a model change clear the selection
and close the view. This rule is new in §6.5 and is recorded in §11.1 along with the two
other decisions the skeleton commits to: the readout's precedence (the open system owns
`#sysread`, and the search result list comes back on close — `searchReadoutShown` is a
flag, not a re-derivation from the result count, so a `matched 0` list also returns) and
the `data-view` gating row, which mirrors the engine rows' `data-engine` mechanism one
level up so a control that means nothing outside its view is hidden rather than disabled.

**The readout writes §6.6's two header lines now** — the star block (designation, class,
mass, luminosity, Teff, radius, age, metallicity) and the geometry line (HZ, snow line,
`a_out`, the system time, planet and belt counts) — at the overlay's 4 Hz cadence, which
is where the scheduler already puts text. The per-planet, belt and delta-v lines are
0.5.3-5, together with `#help`. The `system time` slider is in the menu from now on
because it is the gating mechanism's first consumer and §9.2's only gated row; the rate it
sets is named by the readout immediately, and the phases and belt stipples that follow it,
with the `systime`/`sysview` preset fields of §9.4, are 0.5.3-3.

**Verification.** Headless checks of the layer alone: a closed view issues no 2D calls at
all, `resize` sets the device-pixel store and the transform, an open view clears in CSS
pixels, closing clears in device pixels under the identity transform, and a null context
is tolerated. The readout format was rendered against real `generateSystem` output over a
500-star `MILKY_WAY` field (SBb-style columns, `a_out` compacting at 34 AU, belt and
planet singulars). `python3 scripts/run.py all-tests` → ALL TESTS PASSED;
`m1-smoke-test.js` grew to 61 checks (the `SystemLayer` namespace, the page-scope
definition order, the `#system` canvas above `#labels`, and a regex pin that main.js
consumes both actions and gates the `data-view` row). The run rewrote the date field of 19
log files plus machine-dependent timings in three more; those are reverted so the
committed log set carries only `m1-smoke.json`'s new checks.

Next step: 0.5.3-2, the log-polar layout — `ρ(a)` → device pixels, the habitable-zone
annulus, the orbit ellipses, and the star and planet icons drawn from the record the
skeleton already hands the layer.

## 0.5.3.2 — 2026-10-12 — The log-polar layout: annulus, ellipses, star and icons

0.5.3-2 draws the schematic. `src/render/system-layer.js` gains the radial axis of
§6.1, the habitable-zone annulus, the orbit ellipses and the star and planet icons —
still no renderer, WGSL or record change, and still no main.js change: the layer
reads the `SystemRecord` the skeleton already hands it and everything else is module
constants and preallocated arrays.

**The radial axis.** `a_in_view = max(a_0·0.6, 0.008 AU)` with `a_0` recomputed from
the record's own `rStar` and `state` by the walk's formula (`STAR_CLEARANCE·R★`,
`A0_MIN_AU`, the giant's ×1.5), and `a_out_view = max(a_out·1.2, outermost belt·1.1)`
— the belt factor is in from the start because the record already carries belts, so
0.5.3-3's stipples land inside the span they were drawn for rather than stretching it.
`ρ(a)` maps log `a` into `[R_STAR_PX, R_EDGE_PX]` (34 px … min(w,h)·0.46) and is
clamped to `[0,1]`: a 1000 L☉ giant's habitable edge and its disk edge are close
enough that one side can overshoot.

**Icons, not bodies.** The star is 12/20/6 px by state in the class LUT colour with a
halo at 2×. Planets take the five-bucket ramp on log mass (`M⊕ 0.1/2/20/100 →
3/5/7/10/13 px`), the display-type colour, a 1.7× ring for gas and ice giants, a
half-disc for locked worlds (star-facing half lit) and a green outline for the
terran flag. Ellipses are centred on the star, semi-major the schematic radius,
semi-minor shortened by `e`, rotated by `ω`, squashed by `cos i` — `save`/`rotate`/
`scale`/`ellipse`/`restore` per planet, which is the sentence §6.2 item 2 already
wrote. The per-frame canvas state is one `setTransform(dpr, …)` bookend per group.

**Measured, on a stub-context harness over five real generated systems** (solar, M
dwarf, A star, red giant, white dwarf — the temp script, since the committed
`system-layer-test.js` is 0.5.3-6 with the belts, clock and picker): 47 checks, all
passing — one `clearRect` per frame, both HZ band edges at `r_px(a_hz)` for every
star, one ellipse per planet with semi-major `r_px(a)`, radii ascending with `a` and
inside `[34, 368]`, icon radii only ever the five buckets, and the star icon at its
state's radius under the 2× halo. Frame cost: **19 canvas ops for an empty system,
49 for the solar two-planet two-belt one** — a dozen times cheaper than the label
layer's ~60 `fillText`s.

**Allocation: nothing measurable, and the instrument had to be fixed first.** The
first probe reported 6.6 MB per 1000 draws, which turned out to be the *stub's* own
`[name, args]` pairs (~6 KB/draw). With a recording stub that copies its first four
numeric args into preallocated arrays, 1000 warm draws measured −16 B and +7.8 KB
across trials while the closed-layer control — no drawing at all — measured +48 B and
+8 KB: the layer's delta is inside Node's noise. Recorded in
`findings-pitfalls-skills.md` together with the palette-strings-once rule.

**The page-order smoke test caught three top-level name collisions**, exactly the trap
its 2026-09-20 entry documents: `planetsLib`/`recordLib` (core/search.js's own
functions) and `TAU`/`HALF_PI` (math/orbit.js's constants). Renamed to
`sysPlanets`/`sysRecords` and `SYS_TAU`/`SYS_HALF_PI`; `m1-smoke-test.js` is back to
61/61 and `run.py all-tests` passes. The fixed habit is in the findings file: name
new top-level bindings for the file, not for the thing they hold.

**Deviations from §6.2, recorded here rather than in the plan** (a plan statement, not
a drift): the terran outline sits at `icon radius + 2 px` — the plan names the colour
and width but not the offset, and +2 separates it from the giant ring at 1.7×; the
star halo is the class colour at 0.18 alpha rather than a gradient, because
`createRadialGradient` per frame is a native allocation for a visual difference a flat
disc does not show at 2× a 12 px icon.

Next step: 0.5.3-3, the belt stipples hashed once per system and the system clock —
the layer already holds `systemTimeYr` and the phase draw already reads it, so the
slider's rate lands in one place, and `STIPPLE_N` angle/radius pairs go into the
preallocated `beltStippleXY` arrays this task did not need.

## 0.5.3.3 — 2026-10-13 — The belt stipples, hashed once, and the system clock

0.5.3-3 finishes the moving schematic: the belts of §6.2 item 3 and the separate clock
of §6.4. Still no renderer, WGSL or record change, and the main.js change is the glue
the clock needs — the rate resolution and two preset fields.

**The stipples are hashed once per system, on the belt's own seed.** `setSystem` fills
two preallocated `Float32Array(MAX_BELTS·STIPPLE_N)` — angle and radius — from
`hash01At(belts.stipple[b], 300 + s)`: the first `STIPPLE_N` channels are the dot
angles, the next `STIPPLE_N` the radial offsets, and one channel past them the belt's
phase at `t = 0` (the record carries no belt phase, and a fifth draw per belt keeps the
start angle deterministic without growing the record). A dot sits at
`a_b·exp(dlnA·(2u−1))`, rotated by `phase + TAU·(t/P)` with `P = sqrt(a³/M★)` — the
Kepler period in years with `a` in AU and `M★` in M☉, which are the units §6.4's clock
counts. 0.5.3-2 had already stretched the radial span past the outermost belt
(`BELT_VIEW_OUT`), so the dots land inside the axis they were drawn for.

**The clock is the view's own.** `advance(dt, rate)` accumulates `rate·dt` years only
while the view is open, and main.js resolves the −1 (camera-coupled) rate through the
same `FLIGHT_TIME_GAIN × speedLyPerSec` law the star-time slider uses, one block below
where that law is already written for `dtStar`. `0` freezes; the slider's range
(−1 … 99 y/s) and its default are constants on `SystemLayer`, read by `presets.js`'s
defaults table and clamped by main.js, so no module states the range twice.

**`systime` and `sysview` join the settings line** (§9.4, deferred with the clock):
the rate and the open flag serialize like every other field, both `prompt: false` like
the search query and the selection — the LLM prompt describes the galaxy, not what this
viewer has open. `systime` is a URL parameter too; `sysview` is applied by
`applyPreset` after the selection it needs, since boot restores no selection and a URL
field could only ever name a view with nothing open in it.

**Verified on a stub-context harness over real generated systems** (a two-belt, one-
planet and a belt-free system from `generateSystem`, plus a 13-key scan for both belt
counts): 16 checks, all passing — one `fill` for the whole belt batch, `STIPPLE_N` dots
per belt inside `[R_STAR_PX, R_EDGE_PX]` and each belt's dots inside its own
`a_b·exp(±dlnA)` span, the same system hashing to identical dots in a second layer,
one belt-0 Kepler period of clock returning every dot home to 1e-9 px, a quarter period
rotating it by exactly 90°, rate 0 a true freeze, no dots and no clock while the view
is closed, and a fresh system re-hashing. Frame cost for the two-belt system: **1558
canvas ops** (768 `moveTo`+`arc` pairs and one fill, plus ~22 for the annulus, star and
clear) — within the plan's ~800-op budget shape once the dots' `moveTo`s are counted.
Allocation: five trials of 1000 warm draws with the clock running measured
16 B … 54 KB per trial against 16 B for the closed control, and a 12×5000-draw run
trended flat (no growth after warm-up) — the by-construction no-allocation path inside
Node's GC noise, the same picture 0.5.3-2 recorded.

**Deviations from §6.2 item 3, recorded in §11.1** (plan statements, not drift): the
band is centred on `a_b` in log space rather than the literal `a_b + Δa·u` (the record's
width is `dlnA`, a log half-width); the channel split is angles-then-radii with the
belt phase one channel past the pairs; and the clock stops with the view, so the years a
system accumulates are the years the viewer spent looking at it.

**Verification.** `experiments/preset-test.js` grew to 28 checks (the two fields round-trip,
both stay out of the LLM prompt, the default resolves from `SystemLayer`, and the main.js
wiring pins: the `advance` call, the rate clamp, and the camera-coupled rate law).
`python3 scripts/run.py all-tests` → ALL TESTS PASSED; `m1-smoke.json` records the new
`SystemLayer` API keys and the defaults table's new entry. The run's date/timing churn
across the other 20 logs is reverted, so the committed set carries only the two logs
with new content.
