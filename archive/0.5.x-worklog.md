Two architectural rules are new and are stated as such in §1: planets are **CPU-only** (no
WGSL, no `StarPacked` growth, drawn on a 2D canvas overlay like `label-layer.js`), and
generation is **lazy and never resident** (a system is a pure function of a key, derived on
demand, no cache that grows). The consequence written into the acceptance criteria is that
`wgsl-validate`, `wgsl-exec` and `renderer-test` must not change during this series.

The load-bearing decision is star identity (§2). Selection cannot be a buffer index: the
renderer already documents that *"streaming slots are not stable star identities"*, because
the local gap-fill and catalog blocks are rewritten on every residency change. So every
drawn star gets a stable `u32` key in a parallel `identity` array, and a selection stores a
*snapshot* (key, position, stellar inputs) rather than an index. Stellar inputs come from
the same `deriveStar` call the renderer used for the three procedural blocks (bit-exact) and
from a new `massFromClassAndMag` inversion for landmarks and catalog stars, whose 16-byte
records carry only colour index and absolute magnitude.

Every number the plan pins was computed with the plan's own formulas before being written
down, not quoted from memory — that is what caught five errors in the first draft:

- the Hohmann launch phase angle is `π·(1 − 2·t_H/P2)`, not `π·(1 − t_H/P2)`; the corrected
  form gives 44.4° for Earth→Mars against the published 44°.
- the brachistochrone distance is the half-ellipse arc `π·(a1+a2)/2`, not the radius gap
  `|a2 − a1|`. With the gap, `dv_x` for Earth→Mars came out at 14 km/s — inside a chemical
  tank — and a 1 g ship read 2.1 days. With the arc, `dv_x = 106 km/s`, a chemical hull
  cannot beat the ellipse at all (258.8 d whatever the budget), fusion gives 32.5 d and a
  1 g hull 5.6 d.
- the surface-temperature model needed a mass gate. One greenhouse table made every
  habitable-band world Earth-like, including Mars. With `AIRLESS_M = 0.02 M⊕` and
  `ATM_RETAIN_M = 0.3 M⊕` gating the display type, plus a carbonate-silicate thermostat
  above the gate and a runaway branch (ΔT 500 K, albedo 0.70) above the HZ inner edge, six
  real bodies reproduce from four constants: Mercury 433/440 K, Venus 742/737, Earth
  288/288, Mars 216/210, Moon 270/250, Ceres 163/168.
- ship classes had to satisfy `dv ≥ 2·v_cruise` or the table lies about reaching its own
  cruise speed; fusion and generation were both inconsistent as first drafted.
- and the generation row of the transit table was computed from 0.01 m/s² while §8.1 says
  0.001, which hid a property worth stating: at 0.001 m/s² its ceiling on Earth→Mars is
  48.7 km/s, below the 106 km/s crossover, so a 6 000 km/s tank buys **no** in-system
  speed-up over a 20 km/s chemical hull (258.8 d either way). Correct for the class — it is
  an endurance vehicle whose tank takes 190 years to burn — but only visible if the numbers
  are recomputed rather than reused.

The delta-v module reproduces the published Solar-System map on 19 pins (surface→LEO 9.66
against 9.4, LEO→GEO 3.91 against 3.9, C3=0 escape 3.21 against 3.2, LEO→low Mars orbit
5.04 against ≈5.0, Earth→Mars 258.8 d against 259, surface→surface 15.37 against 15–18),
and two structural invariants on the same real bodies: Earth has a GEO node
(42 163 km < 0.4·r_Hill) and the Moon does not (88 434 km > 24 605 km).

Known limits are recorded in plan §12 rather than left implicit: the HZ inherits
`luminosityFromMass`'s late-M over-luminosity (a TRAPPIST-1 analog's HZ sits 2.7× too far
out — an existing population law, not a new error, and changing it is 0.3/0.4 territory);
the gas-giant mass–radius law is radius-saturating, so Saturn comes out 15% too large;
transfers are coplanar, circular and assist-free; the brachistochrone branch ignores the
destination's orbital velocity and is gated behind `dv_x` for that reason.

**0.5.0-2 — per-record identity.** `star-sprites.js` allocates one `Uint32Array` beside the
staging buffer and every writer fills the slot it wrote: the field's key is
`fieldStarSeed(seed, i)` (`seed*31+i+1`, injective, so the key doubles as the slot), a
landmark's is its table index hashed into `DOMAIN_LANDMARK`, an object member's is the
`objectMemberSeed(objSeed, i)` the writer already computes, the local gap-fill's *is* its
`slotSeed` (so the planet module re-derives it with `imul(key,31)+5` and a position through
`rhoDecomposed`), and a catalog star's is `hash4(DOMAIN_CATALOG, cellId, slotInCell)` —
the payload index, which is what makes the key survive the cell manager's thinning (pinned
by a test that thins the same cells two different ways). `starBlocks()` returns
`{identity, view, blocks, total, dynamicVersion, member}`; `fieldBuffers()` the sampling
arrays the field's derivation reads.

derived with the *object's* coeval age (or as a planetary nebula's central star), and
neither number fits in the 16-byte record. `objects.writeObjectMembers()` now also fills

**The measurement that rewrote §3.1.** The plan assumed a click pass over 300k records costs
~6 Mflop and fits in 16 ms. It is **dispatch-bound, not projection-bound**: the projection is
~15 ns per record, `engineRecordPosition` is ~0.57 µs under the classic mask and ~1.35 µs
with the apocenter pass (its Kepler solve runs for the selected share), so one synchronous
pass is 170–400 ms. The picker is therefore **chunked across frames**: `beginPick` snapshots
the frame (viewProj, camera position, `dynamicVersion`), `pickStep(maxRecords, maxMs)` walks
a slice bounded by both a record count and an 8 ms wall-clock budget, `pickResult()` is the
answer, and a rebuild mid-sweep aborts rather than mixing two frames. `pickAny` stays as the
same walk in one call for tests. Measured at 300k: 10 slices, worst slice 2.7 / 0.5 / 9.8 ms
(and 170 / 32 / 404 ms of work) for the classic / simple / apocenter masks. The plan's
suggested fix — a view-axis pre-reject — was **rejected by the measurement**: it prunes
projections, and projections are 2% of the cost. §12 records the three options if the
sweep's few-frame latency reads as lag on a slower machine, with the ordering argument
(progressive application first, then the *sound* radius-shell prune, then an approximate
pick that reports itself like the simple mask already does).

A second bug came out of the same test: the tie-break, implemented as "compare against the
current best", let a chain of slightly-brighter neighbours walk the pick 4–5 px away from
the cursor (three of the four failures were this). The rule is now anchored on the *nearest*
distance — the winner is the brightest record within `PICK_TIE_PX` of the nearest one — and
the parity check re-derives the winner's dispatched position and holds it to 0.5 px.

512 only (scan-first-512, then nearest-first — not the nearest 512 of all matches). Two
pre-filters sit inside the expensive stage: a slot pre-filter (class/state/component from
the record + cheap channels) and a count pre-check (`planetCount` at the ceiling μ, whose
zero is exact because the chain is monotone in μ and the Hill repair only drops). There
is deliberately no luminosity proxy: the record's byte magnitude already carries
magOffset, so only the derivation knows the true luminosity. Magnitudes and distances
travel as Float64 end to end — a Float32 magnitude rounded the decoded byte just enough
to move mass/luminosity at 1e-7, which the battery's bit-exact props check caught. A set
member whose slot no longer holds its key resolves with kind −1 through the record path
instead of reading another star's component; the barren fast path confirms pre-check
zeroes with no summary and reports `COUNT_UNKNOWN` belts honestly (belts are
summary-computed, and a barren star can hold them). The §5.3 frame-chunked `search.step()`
(0.5.2-5) is not built: every budget passes, and the plan says not to build it before a
measurement says it is needed.
