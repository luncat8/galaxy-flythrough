I have researched the cluster population fractions across galaxy types and cosmic time (galaxy age) and implemented all three star-cluster improvements across the CPU math modules and WGSL shaders:

### 1. Rigid Single-Object Motion (No Shearing into a Curved Line of Stars)
* **Root cause:** Sharing `family` and `jitter` across a cluster's members prevented epicycle-phase boiling at short times, but each member record still evaluated `orbitPosition`, `simplePosition`, and `apocenterPosition` at its own individual birth position $\mathbf{p}_{0,i} = \mathbf{C}_j + \boldsymbol{\delta}_i$. Differential rotation $\Omega(r_i) = v_{\text{flat}} / r_i$ across $r_i = R_c \pm r_{\text{tidal}}$ and individual orbital azimuths $\theta_{0,i}$ sheared each cluster into an arc $20\times\text{–}170\times$ its tidal radius over $2\,000\text{ Myr}$.
* **Fix (`src/math/star-record.js`, `src/math/orbit.js`, `src/render/shaders.js`, `src/render/star-sprites.js`, `src/math/clusters.js`):**
  * Added `FLAG_CLUSTER = 128` (`0x00800000u` in `StarPacked.packed`).
  * Snapped cluster centres to a fine grid $g = 1/32\text{ kpc} = 31.25\text{ pc}$ (`orbit.CLUSTER_GRID_STEP`) inside a $1.0\text{ kpc}$ period ($> 2 \, r_{\text{tidal,max}}$), and packed the 5-bit sub-grid residue into the low 5 mantissa bits of each member's `f32` coordinates (`orbit.packClusterCoord`, $< 0.1\text{ pc}$ perturbation).
  * Both CPU (`orbit.clusterCentreCoord` / `orbit.engineRecordPosition`) and WGSL (`clusterCentreCoord` / `clusterCentre` in `ORBIT` and `SIMPLE_STEP`) recover the exact bit-identical cluster centre $\mathbf{C}_j$ from the 16-byte `StarPacked` record alone, evaluate orbital motion at $\mathbf{C}_j(T)$ across all three movement engines (`classic`, `simple`, `apocenter`), and add back the member's rigid offset $\boldsymbol{\delta}_i = \mathbf{p}_{0,i} - \mathbf{C}_j(0)$. Every cluster now stays within $1.000\times$ its tidal radius at all times ($T = 50, 500, 2000+\text{ Myr}$).

### 2. Cluster Population Split (~75% Halo to ~25% Disk/Bulge Across Galaxies & vs Galaxy Age)
* **Literature & model (`src/math/clusters.js`, `src/math/sampling.js`):**
  * In the Milky Way and average present-day spirals, bimodal globular/bound cluster systems split into **~75% metal-poor halo clusters** and **~25% metal-rich disk/bulge clusters** ([Mackey & van den Bergh 2005](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/355/2/504/970158); [Brodie & Strader 2006 / Peng et al. 2008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.0330); [E-MOSAICS / Kruijssen 2015](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/519/4/5384/6979824)). Because the field stellar halo holds $< 0.1\%$ of the galaxy's stellar mass while hosting ~75% of its surviving clusters (high-redshift proto-galactic formation + long survival in the low-density halo vs rapid GMC/bar tidal disruption in the disc), unweighted field-star sampling previously yielded `0%` halo clusters.
  * Added `clusters.clusterComponentWeights(model)` anchored at `CLUSTER_HALO_FRACTION_REFERENCE = 0.75` and `CLUSTER_HALO_SPECIFIC_FREQUENCY = 1000`, sampling halo cluster positions from `sampling.sampleHaloRadius` + `sampling.sampleDirection`:

### 3. Cluster Orbital Kinematics (Prograde Disk/Bulge vs Broad 3D Halo Directions)
* **Disk & bulge clusters (`src/math/clusters.js`):** In disk galaxies (`vFlat > 0`), `clusterFamily` assigns disk and bulge clusters (`thin`, `thick`, `bulge`) to `FAMILY_PATTERN`, `FAMILY_DISC`, or `FAMILY_BAR` (`ORBIT_INCLINATION_BAR = 0.12` rad $\approx 7^\circ$), so **100%** of disk and bulge clusters follow broadly prograde orbits aligned with the Galactic disk ($L_z = n_z > 0.98$).
* **Halo clusters (`src/math/orbit.js`, `src/render/shaders.js`):** Updated `orbitInclination` for `FAMILY_PRESSURE` from a narrow $\pm 0.30\text{ rad}$ tilt to a full $0..2\pi$ tangent-angle distribution `((phase + rank) / 16) * TAU`, rotating the orbital tangent vector $\mathbf{e}_1 = \mathbf{az}\cos\psi + \mathbf{e}_Z\sin\psi$ around the sphere at each halo cluster's position ([Gaia DR2/DR3 GC kinematics, Vasiliev 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.09824)). Across the Milky Way halo clusters, orbits span **42.5% prograde**, **57.5% retrograde**, and **54.2% high-inclination/polar** ($|L_z| < 0.5$), with net mean angular momentum $|\langle \hat{\mathbf{L}} \rangle| = 0.121$ (no single dominant rotation).
